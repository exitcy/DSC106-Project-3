Climate terms in plain language

You do not need a climate-science background to explore this chart.

Temperature anomaly
A difference, not the actual temperature. For example, +2 °C means 2 °C warmer than the baseline average.
Baseline
The 1995–2014 average used as the chart’s “zero” reference point.
SSP pathway
A possible future based on greenhouse-gas emissions. On this page, SSP1-2.6 is low, SSP2-4.5 is medium, and SSP5-8.5 is high emissions.
Climate projection
A model-based picture of what could happen under a pathway—not an exact weather forecast.
Rolling mean
An average across nearby years that smooths short-term ups and downs and makes the longer trend easier to see.
CanESM5 and CMIP6
CanESM5 is the climate model used for the main chart. CMIP6 is the larger international project that organizes many climate-model experiments.

About This Visualization

Motivation

The central question is how global near-surface temperatures change by 2100 under different greenhouse-gas pathways. That question matters because the same climate model can produce sharply different futures depending on emissions choices, and those differences are hard to judge from a single static number. CMIP6 is appropriate here: it provides publicly archived, scenario-based simulations of near-surface air temperature (tas) that can be compared on a common baseline.

Visual encoding decisions

Position along a shared year axis and a shared temperature-anomaly axis carries the quantitative comparison. Line marks keep the temporal trajectory readable. Scenario identity uses a blue–amber–red palette (low to high emissions) so pathway differences stay distinguishable without inventing a second quantitative channel. A horizontal baseline at 0 °C anchors anomalies to the 1995–2014 reference. Alternatives considered included a dense global grid map (strong for geography, weaker for precise year-to-year comparison) and end-century bars alone (clear ranking, but they hide when pathways diverge). The slider, focus control, and paired comparison are there to surface timing and magnitude of divergence that a static multi-line chart only hints at.

Data transformations

Source: CMIP6 CanESM5 member r1i1p1f1, monthly tas from the public Google Cloud Zarr archive, reduced to annual means and converted from Kelvin to Celsius. Global series are latitude-weighted with the cosine of latitude. Anomalies subtract the CanESM5 historical mean over 1995–2014. Future lines cover 2015–2100 for SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5, and SSP5-8.5; the historical line shows 1850–2014 for context. The summary bars average a 20-year window centered on the selected year and clamp that window to the available 2015–2100 projections. Missing years are dropped rather than filled. The multi-model strip uses each model’s own 1995–2014 baseline for end-century global anomalies. Limitation: the interactive time series is one model (CanESM5), which runs warm relative to the small multi-model set shown below.
